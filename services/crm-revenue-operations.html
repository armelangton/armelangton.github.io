Revenue system

CRM & Revenue Operations

Turn the CRM into a usable operating system for pipeline visibility, consistent execution, accurate reporting, and better revenue decisions.

Quick answer

Palomma improves the structure and workflows around how leads, accounts, opportunities, customers, renewals, and sales activity are managed. The work can include CRM configuration, process design, data cleanup, automation, reporting, and the guidance teams need to use the system consistently.

Why this matters

A CRM only creates value when it reflects the way revenue work actually happens.

When fields are unclear, stages mean different things, ownership is inconsistent, and reports cannot be trusted, the CRM becomes an administrative burden. Good revenue operations connects business definitions, seller workflow, management visibility, and customer lifecycle data into one usable system.

What we improve

Pipeline architecture

Define lifecycle stages, opportunity stages, qualification, ownership, entry and exit criteria, and required next actions.

CRM usability

Simplify layouts, fields, views, queues, tasks, and guidance so users can complete work without unnecessary friction.

Data quality

Clarify sources of truth, standardize critical fields, reduce duplicates, and create practical maintenance rules.

Reporting and forecasting

Build meaningful dashboards and measures around pipeline health, conversion, activity, velocity, coverage, and outcomes.

Common projects

  • Lead, account, contact, opportunity, and customer lifecycle design
  • Sales process and stage definition with clear exit criteria
  • Queue, territory, ownership, assignment, and prioritization logic
  • CRM field, page layout, view, and record simplification
  • Task, reminder, follow-through, and pipeline hygiene workflows
  • Renewal, expansion, and customer transition processes
  • Dashboard, KPI, conversion, and forecast design
  • CRM adoption, training, documentation, and operating routines

How the system is redesigned

  1. 1
    Understand the revenue motion

    Review how demand enters, how opportunities progress, where ownership changes, and what leaders need to know.

  2. 2
    Define the operating model

    Agree on stages, definitions, fields, responsibilities, handoffs, required information, and decision points.

  3. 3
    Configure the CRM

    Implement layouts, validation, workflows, views, queues, automations, reports, and role-based access.

  4. 4
    Prepare data and users

    Clean critical records, document standards, test scenarios, and train the people who use and manage the system.

  5. 5
    Establish the operating rhythm

    Use dashboards, reviews, ownership routines, and continuous improvement to keep the system useful after launch.

Revenue questions the system should answer

  • What needs attention today, and who owns the next action?
  • Where is pipeline moving, stalling, or falling out?
  • Which sources, segments, activities, and approaches produce results?
  • What information is missing before an opportunity can advance?
  • What is likely to close, renew, expand, or become at risk?
  • Where are handoffs or process gaps creating avoidable delays?

Common questions

Do you work with our current CRM?

Yes. The work can support platforms such as Salesforce, HubSpot, Zoho, and other systems when their configuration and access support the required changes.

Do we need to rebuild everything?

No. The best approach is usually to preserve what works, correct the highest-impact structural problems, and sequence changes so the team can adopt them.

Can you help with CRM adoption?

Yes. Adoption is addressed through simpler design, embedded guidance, role-based training, clear ownership, useful reporting, and management routines rather than training alone.

Can AI be added to the CRM workflow?

Yes. AI can support research, summaries, data preparation, recommendations, drafting, and coaching when the underlying data and workflow are defined well enough.
