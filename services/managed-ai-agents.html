AI Assistants

Give your team a reliable assistant for the questions, preparation, research, and repeatable work that slows revenue down.

Quick answer

An AI assistant is built around one clearly defined responsibility. It uses approved business information, follows your rules, and prepares or completes work while keeping people in control of decisions that require judgment.

Why this matters

Make the information your team already has easier to use.

Teams lose time searching across CRM records, documents, notes, inboxes, and shared drives. They also repeat the same research, summaries, and first drafts. A focused assistant brings the right context together, produces a useful starting point, and helps the next action happen faster.

What an assistant can do

Answer questions

Use approved documents and business knowledge to give employees a consistent place to find reliable answers.

Prepare the work

Summarize accounts, opportunities, meetings, requests, documents, or prior activity before a person takes the next step.

Recommend next actions

Use defined criteria and business context to surface what needs attention and suggest a practical next move.

Draft and organize

Create first drafts, structure follow-through, collect missing information, and prepare records for human review.

Common use cases

  • Sales and account research before calls, meetings, or proposals
  • CRM summaries, pipeline context, and next-step guidance
  • Internal knowledge, process, product, and policy questions
  • Document review, comparison, and information extraction
  • Customer or employee request intake and routing
  • Meeting preparation, notes, action items, and follow-through
  • Coaching, onboarding, objection handling, and enablement support

How the assistant is designed

  1. 1
    Define the responsibility

    Choose one repeatable job, the people it supports, and the outcome it should improve.

  2. 2
    Connect trusted context

    Identify the documents, CRM data, systems, instructions, and examples the assistant is allowed to use.

  3. 3
    Set boundaries and review rules

    Define permissions, escalation points, required approvals, and the decisions that remain with people.

  4. 4
    Test with real work

    Evaluate accuracy, usefulness, edge cases, and handoffs before the assistant is released to the team.

  5. 5
    Launch and improve

    Train users, document ownership, monitor performance, and refine the assistant based on actual use.

Good fit and poor fit

A strong fit

The task repeats often, uses identifiable information, has a clear owner, and produces an output a person can evaluate.

Needs more definition first

The goal changes constantly, the source information is unknown, or the assistant would be expected to make high-risk decisions without review.

Common questions

Does an AI assistant replace a person?

The goal is usually to remove repetitive preparation and make information easier to use. People remain responsible for judgment, relationships, exceptions, and approvals.

Can it use our existing documents and CRM?

Yes, when the systems support appropriate access and the information is suitable for the use case. The design defines what the assistant can retrieve, what it can write, and what requires approval.

How do you reduce inaccurate answers?

The assistant is limited to a defined responsibility, grounded in approved context, tested with real examples, and given clear instructions for uncertainty, escalation, and human review.

Can we start with one assistant?

Yes. Beginning with one valuable, measurable responsibility is often the best way to learn what works before expanding.
