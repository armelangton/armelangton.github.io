Orders & Back-Office Workflows

Connect recurring operational steps so routine work moves with less manual coordination and re-entry.

What this solves

Back-office work often crosses multiple people, inboxes, spreadsheets, systems, and approval steps. Palomma can build a bounded workflow that brings the information together, automates repeatable steps, and keeps people involved where exceptions or consequential decisions require review.

Common examples

Order processing

Validate requests, prepare records, route exceptions, and move approved orders into the right system.

Administrative handoffs

Coordinate information and tasks between teams without relying on repeated emails or manual reminders.

Reconciliation and checks

Compare records, identify mismatches, and surface items that need review.

Status and follow-through

Update records, notify owners, create tasks, and keep the process moving after each decision.

How the workflow can work

  1. 1
    Work is triggered

    An order, request, status change, document, or system event starts the process.

  2. 2
    Context is gathered

    The workflow retrieves the records, rules, documents, or history needed for the task.

  3. 3
    Routine steps run

    Automation handles deterministic checks, updates, calculations, routing, and notifications.

  4. 4
    AI assists where useful

    AI can interpret unstructured information, summarize context, classify items, or prepare recommendations.

  5. 5
    People handle exceptions

    Material decisions, uncertainty, or unusual cases are routed for human review.

What can be included

  • Order and request intake
  • Record and document validation
  • Business rules and calculations
  • System-to-system updates
  • Task and approval routing
  • Exception handling
  • Status notifications and reminders
  • Operational reporting or audit context

What a finished implementation looks like

A production workflow that reliably handles the agreed responsibility, integrations, rules, human review points, acceptance criteria, testing, documentation, handoff, and bounded stabilization. The exact architecture depends on the systems and operational risk involved.
