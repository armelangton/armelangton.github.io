Intake & Document Processing

Turn incoming requests and documents into organized, usable information that can move into the right workflow.

What this solves

Teams often spend time opening emails, reading files, copying fields, checking completeness, deciding where something belongs, and re-entering the same information. Palomma can connect those steps so people spend less time on repetitive intake work and more time on the decisions that need judgment.

Common examples

Email and form intake

Capture incoming requests and organize the information needed for the next step.

Document extraction

Identify useful fields, terms, dates, requirements, or other information from approved files.

Validation

Check required information, flag inconsistencies, and route incomplete or unusual items for review.

System entry and routing

Move reviewed information into CRM, operational systems, queues, tasks, or downstream workflows.

How the workflow can work

  1. 1
    Information arrives

    Email, form submission, file, portal entry, or another approved source enters the process.

  2. 2
    Content is interpreted

    AI can extract, classify, summarize, or compare information where flexible understanding is useful.

  3. 3
    Rules are applied

    Required fields, routing logic, thresholds, and deterministic checks keep the process consistent.

  4. 4
    Exceptions are reviewed

    Missing, uncertain, or consequential items are sent to the right person.

  5. 5
    Work moves forward

    Approved information is stored, routed, entered, or used to trigger the next step.

What can be included

  • Email, form, file, and portal intake
  • Document classification and extraction
  • Completeness and validation checks
  • Duplicate or exception detection
  • CRM and business-system entry
  • Task, queue, and owner routing
  • Human review and approval points
  • Status notifications and audit context

What a finished implementation looks like

A working production intake flow with the agreed sources, extraction logic, validation rules, integrations, review points, testing, documentation, handoff, and bounded stabilization. The design depends on the documents, systems, and consequences involved.
