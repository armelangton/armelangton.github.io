Good fit when

  • The process feels harder than it should
  • Work gets stuck or delayed
  • People use too many workarounds
  • Ownership or handoffs are unclear
  • The real cause of the problem is unclear
  • You want to improve the work before adding technology

Process improvement may not be the right fit when

  • The problem and solution are already clear
  • You only need a straightforward system connection
  • You only need a defined workflow automated
  • The issue is a one-time task rather than an ongoing process
  • The process works well and the real constraint is capacity
  • There is no ability to change how the work is done

Common questions

What if we know something is wrong but do not know what is causing it?

That is a good reason to start here. We can examine how the work actually happens, identify the friction, and determine what should change.

Is this just an assessment?

No. Understanding the problem is part of the work, but the engagement can also include redesign and implementation.

Does process improvement always lead to AI or automation?

No. The right answer might be a process change, AI, automation, an integration, better software, or a combination.
