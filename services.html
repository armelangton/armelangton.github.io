AI agents can do important work. They do not have to do all of it.

Palomma builds AI agents and agentic workflows where interpretation, retrieval, language, reasoning, or flexible action improves the workflow. Those capabilities work alongside deterministic rules, existing software, integrations, and people rather than replacing the entire operating process.

Implementation Philosophy

Not every step should be AI.

AI is useful where work requires interpretation, language, retrieval, pattern recognition, or flexible reasoning. Deterministic rules are often better where consistency matters. Existing software should keep doing jobs it already handles well. Human approval belongs where judgment, authority, risk, or consequence makes it appropriate.

How a Project Works

From workflow problem to working implementation.

1

Understand

Define the recurring work, where it slows down, the systems and information it depends on, who owns it, and what better should look like.

2

Design

Define the workflow boundary, integrations, data, rules, AI behavior, approvals, acceptance criteria, and handoff responsibility before the price.

3

Build & Launch

Implement the agreed solution, connect the systems, test the workflow and controls, validate acceptance criteria, and deploy it into production.

4

Handoff & Improve

Stabilize the implementation, document it, make ownership clear, and scope later enhancement, maintenance, or optimization only when useful.

Pricing Approach

Clear scope. Fixed project fee. Production implementation.

Palomma prices production implementations as fixed projects based on the responsibility being implemented—not on billable hours or the number of AI agents involved.

When the workflow and technical environment are sufficiently understood, Palomma can move directly to a fixed implementation proposal. When material uncertainty prevents a responsible quote, a separate fixed-fee scoping engagement may be recommended first. A prototype may be used when there is a genuine feasibility question to answer before production.

What can affect scope

  • Workflow boundary
  • Systems and integrations
  • Data sources and quality
  • Business rules and AI authority
  • Approvals, controls, and evaluation
  • Production environment
  • Documentation and handoff responsibility

No public hourly rates, agent-count pricing, rigid packages, or invented minimums.

I know the business problem but not the solution.

That is enough to start. Describe the workflow and the problem. Palomma determines whether the right architecture uses an agent, automation, integration, retrieval, custom software, or a combination.

We want to use AI but are not sure where it makes sense.

Start with recurring work that consumes time, creates delay, generates rework, or depends on too many manual handoffs. The goal is to identify a viable operational implementation—not to insert AI simply because it is available.
